1. Final Problem Statement and Motivation

1.1 Problem Statement

In modern home entertainment, the rapid expansion of subscription streaming services (Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+ Hotstar, Apple TV+) has caused extreme decision fatigue. Viewers spend between 15 to 20 minutes per viewing session browsing catalogs without reaching a decision.

This problem is compounded by four specific architectural limitations:

  1. Catalog Siloing: Commercial engines are strictly confined to their own licensed libraries and will never suggest matching content hosted on competing platforms.
  2. Algorithmic Opacity (The Black-Box Dilemma): Recommenders utilize complex matrix factorization or deep networks with zero mathematical explainability. Users are offered content without understanding the criteria behind the selection.
  3. Rigid Interfaces & Chatbot Hallucinations: Standard search bars require exact spelling or simple dropdown tags, failing on conversational nuance (e.g., "a rainy psychological thriller with a melancholic mood"). Conversely, general LLMs (like ChatGPT) frequently hallucinate non-existent movie titles, mix up sequels, and provide inaccurate release years.
  4. Fragmented Trailer Discovery & Lack of Unified Video Previews: Currently, movie trailers and teasers are scattered across external video sites and distributor channels. When discovering a movie, viewers must leave the app, open YouTube, dodge clickbait cuts and spoilers, and switch back and forth.

1.2 Motivation

MovieMind bridges this gap by unifying cross-platform discovery, mathematical explainability, and conversational assistance into a single hub. By combining a 19-dimensional genre vector space model (Cosine Similarity) with embedded high-definition official trailers and an orchestrated Google Gemini AI assistant, MovieMind delivers transparent recommendations with verified metadata and zero hallucinations.

3. Background, Context & Literature Review (Recent Research Included)

Recommender Systems (RS) have evolved from classical Matrix Factorization to Content-Based Vector Modeling and modern Tool-Augmented Conversational LLMs. Recent academic literature underscores the necessity of combining generative language models with deterministic domain engines:

Study / Author Focus Area Key Findings & Strengths Identified Limitations Addressed by MovieMind
Salton & McGill (1983) Vector Space Model (VSM) Mathematically proven, bounded ($[0,1]$), low computational complexity $O(n)$ for similarity calculation. Operates on static feature arrays; lacks natural language dialogue capabilities.
Adomavicius & Tuzhilin (2005) Content-Based Filtering Solves collaborative filtering cold-start problems; high item independence without tracking user graphs. Requires structured taxonomies; lacks conversational preference elicitation.
Fan et al. (2023)
IEEE TKDE		 LLMs in Recommender Systems Proves that LLMs excel as conversational orchestrators when strictly paired with external deterministic search/vector tools. Pure LLM prompting without external tools suffers from high token latency and unverified suggestions.
Hou et al. (2024)
ACM TOIS		 Zero-Shot LLM Evaluation Comprehensive benchmark of LLM recommendations; confirms high semantic intent parsing from user queries. Discloses critical **popularity bias and hallucinations** in LLMs unless grounded by verified API catalogs.
Zhang & Chen (2023)
Foundations & Trends in IR		 Explainable AI (XAI) in RS Demonstrates that feature-level explainability (e.g., genre overlap %) significantly boosts user trust and adoption. Black-box neural embeddings hide the rationale behind suggestions, degrading user trust.
Gomez-Uribe & Hunt (2015)
ACM TMIS		 Netflix Industrial Recommender Highly effective deep matrix factorization driving 80%+ of streams within a single streaming provider. Walled garden (only Netflix titles); complete black-box; lacks unified cross-platform discovery.

4. Gap Analysis

The MovieMind Competitive Advantage: Unlike closed streaming platforms that only recommend internal content, and unlike standalone LLMs (like vanilla ChatGPT) that hallucinate non-existent titles, MovieMind couples real-time TMDB metadata with a mathematically grounded Cosine Similarity engine and conversational tool-calling agent.

5. Stakeholders & Requirements Engineering

5.1 Functional Requirements (FR)

  • FR-01 (Live Catalog Retrieval): Dynamic retrieval of trending, top-rated, and now-playing films from TMDB REST API.
  • FR-02 (Title & Keyword Search): Real-time keyword search with instant query results and fuzzy matching.
  • FR-03 (Unified Trailer Playback): Direct high-definition official trailer playback embedded in the movie details modal.
  • FR-04 (Client Watchlist Persistence): Bookmarkable personal watchlist persisted locally via browser Web Storage (LocalStorage).
  • FR-05 (19-Dim Vector Modeling): Automatic formulation of 19-dimensional genre vectors matching standard TMDB categories.
  • FR-06 (Cosine Similarity Scoring): Real-time vector dot product ranking against the user's taste profile centroid.
  • FR-07 (Conversational Dialogue): Multi-turn natural language conversational chat with structured card rendering.

5.2 Non-Functional Requirements (NFR)

  • NFR-01 (Performance & Latency): Vector math computation latency < 100ms; total conversational response < 3.0s.
  • NFR-02 (Security & Isolation): Zero API key leakage; all secret keys isolated on serverless environment variables.
  • NFR-03 (Fault Tolerance): Automatic failover between primary Gemini Flash and secondary fallback models.
  • NFR-04 (Privacy by Design): Client-side taste storage with zero invasive tracking or telemetry logging.

9. Proposed Solution and Detailed Methodology

9.1 Five-Stage Engineering Methodology

Stage 1
Data Ingestion
Real-time REST API fetching from TMDB (/trending, /now_playing, /videos). Payload normalization into standardized JSON records.
Stage 2
Vector Modeling
Mapping categorical movie genres into 19-dimensional binary vectors V_M ∈ {0,1}^19 corresponding to TMDB taxonomy.
Stage 3
Cosine Similarity
Formulating user taste centroid U across watchlist films. Calculating normalized dot product similarity against candidate pools.
Stage 4
AI Orchestration
Google Gemini Flash orchestrated via LangChain tool calling (recommend_movies, search_movies) with Zod structured output.
Stage 5
Media Previews
Embedding official YouTube HD trailers directly into movie modal. Client-side LocalStorage persistence for privacy sovereignty.
Verification
Explainability
Displaying exact Cosine Similarity percentage (e.g., 82% Match) alongside natural language reasons grounded in genre DNA.

9.2 Mathematical Formulation (Cosine Similarity Engine)

Each film is mapped to a 19-dimensional binary vector $\vec{V}_M$ matching standard TMDB genre IDs. The user profile $\vec{U}$ is computed as the normalized centroid of liked films. The similarity score is computed as:

Cosine Similarity(U, V) = ( U · V ) / ( ||U|| × ||V|| ) = ∑(U_i × V_i) / ( √∑(U_i)² × √∑(V_i)² )

Candidate films are sorted in descending order of similarity ($S \in [0.0, 1.0]$), filtering out titles already consumed by the user.
